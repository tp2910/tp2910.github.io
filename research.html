Research

My work develops computational and probabilistic methods for engineering systems where uncertainty is not just a nuisance but a central design consideration. The common thread is rigorously quantifying what we do not know, and using that knowledge to make better decisions under uncertainty — whether in the design of a new aircraft, the planning of a test programme, or the evaluation of a novel propulsion technology.

Uncertainty Quantification

I develop end-to-end UQ frameworks that propagate aleatory and epistemic uncertainties through high-fidelity aerospace simulations. This includes constructing surrogate models to make Monte Carlo analysis tractable, applying polynomial chaos expansion and sparse quadrature methods, and performing global sensitivity analysis (Sobol indices, Morris screening) to identify which design variables and component uncertainties drive system-level risk. Current application areas include hybrid-electric propulsion, battery degradation, and sustainable aviation fuel combustion.

Multidisciplinary Design Optimization

Engineering systems of practical interest — aircraft, propulsion architectures, energy systems — involve coupled, competing disciplines. I work on MDO methods that properly account for uncertainty at the system level, including reliability-based design optimization (RBDO) and optimization under uncertainty (OUU). This requires integrating gradient-based and evolutionary optimizers with probabilistic constraint handling, and efficiently computing sensitivities across disciplines using adjoint methods.

Sustainable Aviation

Decarbonising aviation requires navigating a wide set of technological pathways, each with its own uncertainty profile. My research examines the full landscape: hybrid-electric and fully electric propulsion, sustainable aviation fuels (SAF) from biomass, waste streams, and synthetic (power-to-liquid) routes, hydrogen propulsion, and operational efficiency measures. A consistent thread is that quantifying the uncertainty and risk in each pathway is essential for sound technology selection, investment prioritisation, and certification planning. I am particularly interested in how probabilistic methods can accelerate the readiness of these technologies.

Technology Maturation and Risk Analysis

Novel aerospace technologies face the challenge of demonstrating readiness through a limited test budget. My dissertation introduced two metrics — Failure Impact (FI) and Uncertainty Impact (UI) — that extend beyond traditional Technology Readiness Level (TRL) assessments to capture how component-level failures and uncertainties cascade to the system level. Built on these metrics is a multi-attribute decision-making framework for prioritising testing, together with an adaptive resource allocation procedure that updates test plans as new data arrive.

Machine Learning for Engineering Simulation

Many UQ and MDO workflows require thousands of solver evaluations, making high-fidelity simulation prohibitive. I build surrogate models — Gaussian processes, neural networks, radial basis functions — and physics-informed machine learning frameworks that make these analyses tractable while preserving physical fidelity. A particular interest is active learning: selecting the most informative design points to query so that surrogates can be built with the fewest expensive simulations.
